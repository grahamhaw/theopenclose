## Opening

**Exploratory talks — SK Hynix may manufacture memory on U.S. soil with Intel for the first time.**
[CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/intel-sk-hynix-us-memory-chips.html) reports, citing Reuters, that SK Hynix is in talks with Intel about U.S. memory manufacturing. Under one scenario Hynix would lease part of Intel's Ohio facility; Reuters also said the companies could form a joint venture with Intel and major cloud firms. Talks are exploratory and no decisions have been made; neither company immediately commented to CNBC. Shares of both rose in premarket on the report.

## The numbers

**$4B Indiana HBM plant — prior U.S. plan, not this Intel deal.**
CNBC notes SK Hynix broke ground last month on a $4 billion Indiana facility that CEO Kwak Noh-Jung said would make the state a "key HBM production base in America" by 2030 — a separate, already-announced path.

**Capacity doubling over five years — June company plan.**
CNBC cites SK Hynix's June plan to double capacity over five years, and Chairman Chey Tae-won telling CNBC in July that HBM demand is "enormous."

## Why it matters

A foundry/lease or cloud-inclusive JV would be a new U.S. memory supply path for HBM and other AI memory, alongside Hynix's Indiana build — still talks, not wafers. CNBC/Reuters flag possible South Korean government sensitivity if advanced memory like HBM is involved.

## Positioning

**HBM supply binds** — Onshoring talks do not by themselves loosen near-term HBM tightness; process transfer, tools, and yield remain the slow steps if the structure is HBM-related. A hyperscaler-inclusive JV framing (visible in market discussion) would bear on who controls incremental memory supply if talks firm.
