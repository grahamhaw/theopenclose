## Opening

**Anthropic named Accenture’s Faculty unit its first embedded frontier evaluator — the first concrete step on Amodei’s “pace the frontier” evaluator commitment.**
In a [Friday company post](https://www.anthropic.com/news/accenture-embedded-evaluation), Anthropic says Faculty will red-team models, run alignment assessments, and test safeguards with access “comparable to an employee’s,” watching training and deployment decisions from inside. Anthropic and Accenture each “expect to invest at least $1 billion” building capacity in this area over five years; given “importance and urgency,” Anthropic will fund Accenture’s work directly. The deal is non-exclusive; Anthropic says it is in dialogue with METR and other nonprofit evaluators under different funding arrangements, and that embedded evaluators do not reduce its own accountability.

## The numbers

**≥$1 billion each over five years — Anthropic (company claim; capacity build, not a disclosed cash schedule).**

**Anthropic funds Accenture’s evaluator work directly for now — Anthropic; long-term preferred pooled/government funding does not exist yet (same post).**

## Why it matters

Operationalizes the first leg of Amodei’s pacing proposal (employee-like third-party access with publish rights subject to narrow redactions). Tier-1 tape overnight treated the “independent” label as contested: [@firstadopter](https://x.com/firstadopter/status/2101179010784940302) called a consulting giant funded by the issuer a conflict of interest; [@Klaudnin3](https://x.com/Klaudnin3/status/2101161565424586790) analogized issuer-picked raters to subprime AAA stamps. That critique is about governance design, not about whether Faculty will sit inside the building.

## Positioning

**Enterprise agent stall** — Embedded eval is Anthropic’s answer to verifiability of safety/pacing claims; the falsifier for the theme remains a Fortune 500 naming a production agent with headcount or dollars — not whether an evaluator contract exists.
