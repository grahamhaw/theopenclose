## Opening

**Samsung has moved wafer-to-wafer hybrid bonding (B1b) onto its formal next-gen DRAM roadmap as the D0a label, demoting VCT DRAM to a separate D0a-V track, per ZDNet Korea.**
[ZDNet Korea](https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20260923090551) (Jang Kyung-yoon, Sep 23) reports B1b had been developed as a project but was not on the formal roadmap; it is now named as the first sub-10 nm-class DRAM node. Previously D0a referred to vertical-channel-transistor (VCT) DRAM. The paper frames the rename as accelerating advanced packaging into commodity DRAM: cell and peri on separate wafers, bonded Cu-to-Cu. Sources cited say commercialization hurdles for B1b look lower than VCT, and that Samsung is prioritizing B1b. Surfaced overnight by [@jukan05](https://x.com/jukan05/status/2102642359687401795).

## The numbers

**D0a label — reassigned from VCT to B1b (W2W hybrid bonding); VCT → D0a-V — ZDNet Korea / sources.**
**Commercialization of B1b-based D0a — earliest ~2 years; mass-production investment schedule expected mid next year — ZDNet Korea / industry.**
**HBM5 — Samsung reviewing B1b attach; mass production expected ~2029; target ~50%+ higher operating speed vs HBM4E — ZDNet Korea.**
**Prior nodes (marketing class) — 1c ~11–12 nm; 1d ~10–11 nm as last 10 nm-class — ZDNet Korea.**

## Why it matters

Roadmap timing for the packaging step that lifts DRAM density without waiting on harder VCT cell change. If B1b also lands in HBM5, hybrid bonding becomes a shared platform across commodity DRAM and stacked AI memory rather than NAND/CIS-only.

## Positioning

**HBM supply binds** — Does not loosen near-term HBM wafer scarcity; shifts which process Samsung prioritizes for sub-10 nm / HBM5 density. Binding constraint stays capacity and stack yield until HBM5 prints.
