## Opening

**Rhodium puts a hard ARR gap under China vs U.S. frontier labs — usage ≠ revenue.**
[CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/17/chinas-ai-models-make-only-10percent-of-us-leaders-revenue-rhodium.html) (Evelyn Cheng) reports Rhodium Group estimates that all Chinese AI models combined generate only about 10% of the revenue reported for OpenAI and Anthropic, using annual recurring revenue (ARR) constructed from recent monthly figures ×12.

## The numbers

**Chinese AI models combined ≈ 10% of OpenAI + Anthropic revenue — Rhodium via CNBC.**
**DeepSeek ARR $500 million; MiniMax $800 million; Moonshot $1 billion — Rhodium via CNBC.**
**Z.ai latest ARR $1.8 billion (company to investors Wed); year-end ARR target raised to $3 billion from $2.4 billion — CNBC.**
**ByteDance $4 billion; Alibaba $2.4 billion — Rhodium via CNBC.**
**OpenAI $40 billion ARR; Anthropic $65 billion — Rhodium via CNBC.**
**Valuation/revenue ratios cited: Moonshot ~50x, DeepSeek ~163x vs OpenAI ~34x, Anthropic ~21x — Rhodium via CNBC.**
**>60% of equity investment in Chinese AI chips/servers from state-affiliated sources — Rhodium via CNBC.**

Rhodium partner Logan Wright (to CNBC): financing gap makes sustainable scale harder for Chinese frontier labs; equity-market dependence; government funding more hardware- than lab-focused.

## Why it matters

Separates open-weight adoption / token volume narratives from dollar ARR and financing capacity — relevant when comparing China labs’ “catch-up” stories to U.S. closed-model revenue bases ahead of listing talk.

## Positioning

**Frontier lab monetization vs open weights** — Low ARR relative to usage is a feature of open distribution and lower task pricing (CNBC cites Artificial Analysis on cost-per-task), not proof of capability parity in dollars.
