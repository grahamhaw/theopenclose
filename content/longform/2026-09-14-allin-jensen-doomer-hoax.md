## Abstract

Jensen Huang, founder/CEO of NVIDIA (accelerators, networking, and AI-factory systems), sits with Chamath Palihapitiya, Jason Calacanis, David Sacks, and David Friedberg for a live All-In Summit session (~47m; YouTube: [Jensen Huang: The Doomer Hoax…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S7CrlFLAmEA)). Ground covered: Dario Amodei / frontier-lab slowdown and 'doomer' prediction scorekeeping; regulation vs root-cause engineering and multi-auditor evals; recursive self-improvement as ordinary tooling; Hugging Face / open vs closed models and China open-source volume; a mid-session speakerphone call from President Trump on data-center permitting; NVIDIA capital allocation across supply chain and NeoClouds; open-stack ambitions (Nemotron, Alpamayo, biology models); and a brief take on Elon's Terafab.

## Theses

Failed AI-apocalypse timelines are Huang's accountability wedge against extinction-probability rhetoric — radiology job wipeout, '90% of code in 6–12 months,' and '50% of entry jobs in 6–9 months' are named as wrong, so '10% extinction' is treated as unscientific rather than precautionary.
Huang: predictions 'proven to be wrong'; quantification is 'made up' and 'irresponsible'; whistleblowing (Coxin) is separated from the scientific claim. [book]

Safety vs American AI leadership is framed as a false choice: actual incidents concentrate at frontier labs because they hold the most compute, so the prescription is root-cause engineering, voluntary pause only if a lab is out of control, and multiple independent evaluators — not a slowdown.
Huang: 'Slowing down is definitely the wrong strategy'; auditors/evaluators should be plural so 'one company doesn't become… pilled.' [book]

Open models are load-bearing for the application layer: ~$400bn of AI-native venture funding in ~6 months, with ~80% said to use open models; closed models are 'bottled water' — both are needed for sovereignty, privacy, and proprietary stacks.
Huang cites the funding mix as the reason founders can build dreams that differ from frontier labs'; China is the majority contributor to open-source volume today, but forks make downloaded models 'yours.' [book]

NVIDIA's commercial posture is enablement depth, not lab capture: 'go up as far as we need to and as low as possible,' with the platform claimed to have broadened from running mainly OpenAI ~18 months ago to Meta/Grok/Gemini and Anthropic scaling on-stack.
Huang: prefer helping 'everybody succeed instead of taking a slice'; NeoClouds exist because hyperscaler annual planning is 'always almost wrong' on land/power/shell. [book]

Narrow superintelligence is already here in Huang's definition — self-driving at far-below-human accident rates and protein synthesis / virtual screening — while China native advanced lithography is dated to ~2030; Terafab is shrugged as feasible if anyone can do it.
Huang: 'I think we're already there' on AGI-as-human-parity in narrow domains; lithography 'by 2030'; Terafab 'if anybody could do it, he can.' [book]

Trump's live call is market-relevant only insofar as it hardens a federal pro-build narrative against data-center permitting pushback (Google Finland cited as permitting failure) and brands opposition a 'hoax,' with data centers called 'the oil of the next 20 25 years.'
Trump: '$20 trillion' investment claim vs '<1 trillion' prior-term contrast — political arithmetic, not NVIDIA guidance; Huang after the call stresses jobs / re-industrialization / energy as the bridge he wanted on the call.
