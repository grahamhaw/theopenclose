## Opening

**Goldman’s clearability math — ~$1.42T cumulative revenue by 2030 / ~$11.6B per GW for 15% ROIC, with 59% already in cloud backlogs — has the better of pure “capex is doomed” overnight takes, but the same thread’s sensitivity table ($920B/yr even at 0 ROIC) and rate path keep the break conditions live.**
@zerohedge overnight TL/DR of a Goldman spreadsheet on justifying ~$1.7T hyperscaler capex ([post](https://x.com/zerohedge/status/2103665734677106804); [earlier link post](https://x.com/zerohedge/status/2103553902876459476)); counter in the same note lists the ifs (compute stays scarce, GPU/token prices don’t deflate, 5-year useful life, labs keep paying, $3T off-balance-sheet needs its own return). A paired ZH post says Goldman’s sensitivity table shows that if hyperscaler ROIC on capex is **0**, they still need **$920BN every year** for depreciation and running costs ([post](https://x.com/zerohedge/status/2103647140463395173)). Tier-1 replies reframes: @Klaudnin3 maps the same math to an Apple “rent the GW” bull vs shovel owners; @jukan05 treats elevated rates as a major obstacle to AI investment even while quoting a Fed Jul-2000 line that tech capex can hold up vs borrowing costs; @TheStalwart asks why META would pay *more* interest to stay nominally asset-light. Deep reply tails on Stalwart/jukan unread (`top-replies`). Numbers are Goldman-via-ZH / sell-side-via-X — not a primary GS PDF in this capture.
